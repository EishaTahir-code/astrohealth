# Board: Orbloom

**Vote: 2 pass, 1 fund if. Average score 3.3 / 10** (Offers 3 PASS, Monopoly 3 PASS, Product 4 FUND IF).

The board scored Orbloom as a business, not as a hackathon entry. All three members called the build careful and the offline-first, privacy-first design right for the problem. None of them could fund it yet, for the same reason: nobody who would use it or pay for it has seen it.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **No proof from real users** (all three). No astronaut, analog crew or flight surgeon has used it. Every moment of use is a guess, and a buyer whose crew's health is at stake has no reason yet to believe it works.
2. **Nobody knows what crews use today** (all three). Without that, nobody can say what Orbloom replaces, why it is better, or whether it sits beside existing tools.
3. **No buyer, no price, no route to them** (Offers, Monopoly). The buyers are a short list of agencies, station operators and analog programmes. How many there are, who signs, what they spend and how long a sale takes are all unknown.
4. **Alert thresholds are general ranges** (Offers, Product). Red alerts use general reference ranges, not each astronaut's normal, and no flight surgeon has reviewed them.

Raised by one member only:
- **Nothing to stop a copy** (Monopoly). Common tools on free tiers plus public NASA data; an agency's own team could rebuild it. No network effect, scale or brand yet.
- **No one thing** (Product). Nine astronaut features; the greenhouse and medical kit read as answers to the hackathon's NASA-data rule more than to a crew member's day.
- **Seams** (Product). Family replies are placeholders; the help signal waits on the same link that drops; a wilting flower bed that prompts crewmates could feel like being watched.

## Where they disagree

- **Market.** The Offers lens calls the market weak (few, slow buyers). The Monopoly lens likes a small starting market, provided it turns out to be real.
- **Fixable or not.** The Product lens would fund it if the team narrows it and tests it with real people. The other two want outside evidence (a committed pilot, a count of buyers) before they reopen the vote.

## Conditions (merged checklist)

- [ ] Write Orbloom in one sentence and cut the app down to what serves it. (Product)
- [ ] Map what agencies and analog programmes use today for crew and behavioural health, and where Orbloom fits next to it. (all three; `/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] Run sessions with flight surgeons and at least one analog crew, and write up their first five minutes from what they actually did. (Product)
- [ ] Get one named analog programme or flight surgeon to commit to a pilot, paid or formally agreed. (Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] Count the buyers: how many long-duration and analog crews start each year, who signs off and what they spend on crew-health tools. (Offers, Monopoly; open question, no skill can invent it)
- [ ] Have a flight surgeon review the alert thresholds, ideally per person rather than general. (Offers, Product)
- [ ] Close the visible seams: real family replies or none, a clear waiting state for the help signal. (Product)
- [ ] Find know-how a copycat cannot take: surgeon-checked protocols, data from real pilots. (Monopoly)
- [ ] Set a price for a pilot and say what you would guarantee (for example, every alert delivered once the link returns). (Offers; `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-cfo`)

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from the pitch. The strongest Orbloom is narrower: **an offline-first health log that gets a crew's red flags to their flight surgeon even when the link to Earth is down, without showing the surgeon anything private.** That is the part all three members praised, and the part no ordinary health app does. The first market would be analog-mission programmes (Mars and Moon simulation habitats), which are reachable and run missions on Earth, so a pilot there is cheap and quick compared with spaceflight. The alertness game and the check-in would stay as the inputs that feed that link. The greenhouse, the crew garden and family messages would wait until a crew asks for them. The value would be proven with flight-surgeon-reviewed thresholds and one real pilot, not with more features.
