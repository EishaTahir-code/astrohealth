# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- It starts in a small market: crews on long and analog missions, and their flight surgeons. This lens wants a small starting market, provided the market turns out to be real.
- The design treats the hard constraint as the product. It is offline-first, encrypts data on the device, syncs when a link opens and queues help signals for the next window. The team's own problem statement notes that most health apps assume constant connectivity.
- The privacy split could win crews' trust. The flight surgeon never sees moods, check-ins or family messages, and every visit is logged. In a community this small, that trust could grow into a brand.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. Nothing proprietary. The stack is common tools on free tiers, and the content is public NASA data (the ISS medical kit list and the ISS plant research). It was built during a hackathon, so a copycat or an agency's own staff could rebuild it just as fast. Nobody can say yet whether it is ten times better than anything, because nobody knows what it would replace: the tools agencies already use are an open fact.
2. No distribution. So far there have been no buyer conversations, no interviews with astronauts or flight surgeons, and no price. The buyers are a handful of agencies, station operators and analog programmes. How many of them exist, how they buy and what one sale costs are all unknown.
3. No moat that grows. There is no network effect across organisations (the crew garden links one crew, not buyers), no economy of scale and no brand yet. The brief gives no reason the position would hold for ten years against in-house builds or existing vendors.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do agencies and analog programmes use today for crew and behavioural health, and on what is it ten times worse than Orbloom?
- How many buyers exist, who signs off, and what does winning the first one cost in time and money?
- What do you know about this problem that an agency's own team does not?
- Why now?
- Which small piece could you own outright first, and what is the next market after it?
- What stops a copycat, or a buyer's in-house team, in month six?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable, because the vote is PASS. Three pieces of evidence together would reopen it: one named buyer willing to run a pilot, a clear account of the tools it would replace, and know-how competitors cannot copy (for example, protocols checked by flight surgeons and data from real pilots).
