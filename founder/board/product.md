# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- It is built around the real constraint. Working offline first, with data stored on the device and synced when a link opens, fits a link that can take about 20 minutes each way.
- There is care in the parts nobody sees. Check-ins stay private, the flight surgeon never sees moods or family messages, every surgeon visit is logged, the AI never diagnoses, red flags skip the AI, and the medical kit has no AI on purpose.
- It is live, and the sample crew makes the first five minutes easy to try.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing.** The astronaut side has nine features. Describing it takes a paragraph. The greenhouse and medical kit look like answers to the hackathon's NASA-data rule, not to a crew member's day.
2. **It was built from the inside out.** Nobody has talked to an astronaut, an analog crew or a flight surgeon. Every moment of use is a guess, and what crews use today is unknown.
3. **Things break at the handoffs.** Family replies are placeholders. The help signal travels over the very link that drops. Red alerts use general ranges, not each astronaut's own normal. A wilting flower bed that prompts crewmates could feel like being watched.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Can you say what Orbloom is in one sentence?
- On day 90 of a mission, when does an astronaut open it, and why?
- If you could keep only one feature, which would it be, and what evidence points to it?
- When a help signal is waiting for the link, what does the astronaut see and do?
- Who sets the alert thresholds, and how would a flight surgeon learn to trust them?
- What do crews use today, and does Orbloom replace it or sit beside it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Write the one sentence and cut the app down to what serves it. Put the rest away until a user asks for it.
2. Use the introductions the team asked for to run sessions with flight surgeons and at least one analog crew. Bring back a first-five-minutes account written from what they did.
3. Close the visible seams: no placeholder replies, a clear waiting state for the help signal, and thresholds reviewed by a flight surgeon.
4. Map the tools crews already use and where Orbloom fits next to them.
