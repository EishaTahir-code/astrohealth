# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The dream outcome is large and easy to explain: catch a crew member's slow decline before it becomes a mission problem, with no doctor on board and a link that drops out.
- Time to value is short. It installs, works offline and gives the astronaut a result on day one (a check-in, a reading against a range, an alertness score).
- The privacy split (the surgeon never sees moods or family messages, and every visit is logged) answers an objection a crew would raise. That is a real piece of a stack.
- It is built and live for close to no money, so testing it with a real crew costs very little.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market.** The Offers lens judges the market first, and this one is weak on the brief's own facts. The buyers are a short list of agencies, station operators and analog programmes. Nobody knows how many crews there are, what they spend or how long they take to buy. The buyers are easy to name but probably slow and hard to sell to. Whether the market is growing is not in the brief.
2. **Likelihood: no proof.** No astronaut or flight surgeon has used it. The ranges are general ones, not built for people in space, and the AI and storage run on free consumer tiers. For a buyer whose crew's health is at stake, the chance it works as promised looks close to zero until someone credible vouches for it.
3. **Effort for the buyer, and no offer yet.** An agency would face security review, medical sign-off and fitting it alongside the tools it already uses, which the brief does not name. There is no price, no named buyer, no guarantee and no clear one-line promise, so nothing exists yet to judge as an offer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who signs the cheque at an analog programme, and have you talked to one?
- How many long-duration and analog crews start each year, and what does each spend on crew health tools today? (open question)
- What do flight surgeons use now, and what would make them switch?
- What would a flight surgeon need to see before trusting a red alert from this app?
- What could you guarantee, such as a set amount of offline uptime or every alert delivered once the link returns?
- If an analog programme paid for a pilot, what would it cost them, and could you double that price and still be the obvious pick?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable. The vote is PASS. Two things would reopen it: one analog programme or flight surgeon agreeing to a paid or formally committed pilot, and a rough count of buyers and their budgets showing a market worth winning.
