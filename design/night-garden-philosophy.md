# Nocturne Herbarium

*Design philosophy for Orbloom's visual identity, written with the canvas-design skill.
Sources: `garden.png` (luminous wildflowers in darkness) and `moodboard.png` (orbital navy and teal, leaf green, pale lime, annotated specimens).*

Light is the only flower that grows in the dark. Nocturne Herbarium treats a meadow at night the way an old botanical atlas treats a pressed specimen: held still, lit from within, and measured. Every bloom is a small source of light set against an enormous, patient darkness. The darkness is never empty black. It is a deep orbital navy that thins into teal haze near the horizon, the colour of a planet's edge seen from very far away. The work should feel meticulously crafted, every stem drawn with painstaking attention, as if a master illustrator had spent a season on a single plate.

Form comes from growth, never from decoration. Stems rise in long, unhurried curves; flower heads are built from repeated perfect marks: petals as ellipses, umbels as rings of dots, seed heads as radiating hairlines. Repetition is the method. Hundreds of small, nearly identical marks accumulate into something that looks alive. Nothing is filled flat; light gathers at edges and centres, and the eye reads glow before it reads shape. The result must look like the product of deep expertise, the labour of countless refinements rather than a quick effect.

Colour is a strict, quiet system. Navy holds the ground. Teal carries air and distance. Leaf green lives only in stems and leaves. Pale lime is reserved for living light: pollen, fireflies, the single signal that matters. White is used like a precious metal, for petals at full bloom and nowhere else. No colour appears without a job, and the palette stays restrained enough that one lime point can command a whole plate.

Space is generous and vertical. The lower third grows; the upper two thirds breathe. A planet's limb may cross the sky as a single fine arc of rim light, a reminder of where this garden actually is. Scale contrast does the storytelling: vast sky, tall stems, tiny luminous detail that rewards close looking. Composition is balanced but never symmetrical, like a real field.

Text is a specimen label, never a headline. Where words appear inside the artwork they are tiny, monospaced, bracketed annotations joined to their subject by hairline leader lines, the notation of an imaginary discipline that studies how well-being blooms. They whisper. Everything sits inside the frame with clear margins; nothing overlaps, nothing is crowded. The finished plate should look master-level in its execution: a pristine artefact proving that something as quiet as a person's well-being can be observed with care.
